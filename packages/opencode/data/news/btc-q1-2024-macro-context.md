## 1. Overall BTC Price Trend & Volatility Regime

Bitcoin posted a **+68.8% return** in Q1 2024, making it one of the strongest first quarters on record (4th best since 2013, 2nd best since 2013 excluding the early years). The quarter was characterized by **extreme volatility with three distinct phases**:

| Phase                           | Dates           | Price Range                     | Return                      |
| ------------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Pre-ETF rally & "sell the news" | Jan 1 – Jan 23  | $42,258 → $39,505 (low)         | −6.5% (peak to trough −16%) |
| Consolidation & basing          | Jan 24 – Feb 6  | $39,505 – $44,341               | ~+12%                       |
| ETF-driven rally to new ATH     | Feb 7 – Mar 13  | $42,658 → $73,098               | +71%                        |
| ATH correction & recovery       | Mar 14 – Mar 31 | $60,808 (low) → $71,247 (close) | −18% trough, +17% recovery  |

**Volatility metrics:**

- Implied volatility (IV) for BTC options surged from ~30% (Oct 2023) to **over 97%** during ETF launch week (Jan 11).
- Daily average spot trading volume reached **$34.1B** (+89.8% vs Q4 2023's $18.0B).
- 90-day annualized volatility declined structurally from 150%+ in earlier cycles to below 50% in 2023-24, suggesting a maturing market.
- After ETF launch, a disproportionate share of volume concentrated in the 3-4 PM ET ETF fixing window (10-13% of daily volume vs <5% previously), which may dampen intraday volatility.

**Verdict on trading regime:** The quarter was **strongly trending (bullish)** with two sharp mean-reverting episodes (Jan 12-23 sell-off, Mar 15-20 ATH correction). The dominant feature was a persistent uptrend fueled by relentless ETF inflows. For a Bollinger Bands mean-reversion strategy, opportunities existed primarily during the post-ETF sell-off consolidation (late Jan) and the ATH correction (mid-Mar), but the prevailing trend regime would have tested mean-reversion stops frequently during Feb-Mar.

## 3. Market Behavior: Trending, Ranging, or Mean-Reverting?

The quarter can be assessed in **three behavioral regimes**:

### Phase 1: Mean-Reverting / "Buy the Dip" (Jan 12 – Feb 6)

- Post-ETF sell-off created a -16% drawdown from $49k to $39.5k.
- Price ranged between $39,500 and $44,000 for ~3 weeks.
- Net ETF flows were negative some days (GBTC outflows > New Nine inflows).
- **Best period for mean-reversion strategies**: clear support at $39,500-$40,000; resistance at $44,000.

### Phase 2: Strong Trending (Feb 7 – Mar 13)

- BTC rallied from ~$42,700 to $73,098 — a **+71% move in 5 weeks**.
- Nearly uninterrupted: consecutive green weekly candles.
- ETF inflows were relentless (IBIT had 69 consecutive days of inflows).
- **Worst period for mean-reversion**: trend-following strongly outperformed. Bollinger Bands would have been consistently breached to the upside.

### Phase 3: Corrective / Consolidating (Mar 14 – Mar 31)

- ATH at $73,098 on Mar 13; sharp -18% correction to $60,808 on Mar 20.
- ETF inflows slowed to $4.6B in March (vs $6B in February).
- After the Mar 20 FOMC low, BTC recovered back to ~$71,247 by month-end.
- **Moderate mean-reversion opportunity**: the drop below $65k on Mar 19-20 was sharp and reversed quickly.
- By end of quarter, daily chart showed a 20-bar sideways range — no clear directional bias.

### Summary for strategy purposes

| Metric                           | Assessment                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trend regime**                 | Strongly bullish overall (Q1 return +68.8%)                                                                                                                                      |
| **Mean-reversion opportunities** | 2 episodes: Jan 12-23 sell-off, Mar 15-20 ATH correction                                                                                                                         |
| **Volatility regime**            | High; IV tripled from 30% to 97%+ around ETF launch; daily ranges expanded                                                                                                       |
| **Bollinger Band behavior**      | Bands widened significantly; price hugged upper band during Feb-Mar rally; mean reversion only worked at local extremes                                                          |
| **Key takeaway**                 | Q1 favored trend-following. Mean-reversion entries would have worked at the Jan low (~$39.5k) and Mar 20 low (~$60.8k). Between those extremes, shorting strength was dangerous. |

## 6. Implications for a Bollinger Bands Mean-Reversion Strategy

Based on this Q1 2024 review, key design considerations for `btc-bollinger-reversion`:

1. **ETF inflow data is the dominant price driver.** During periods of strong ETF inflows (Feb-Mar), mean-reversion shorts against the upper band would have suffered. Consider incorporating ETF flow data as a filter or regime classifier.

2. **Two clean mean-reversion setups existed:**
   - **Jan 23 sell-off low** (~$39.5k): Touch of −2SD after 16% decline. Perfect reversion entry — BTC rallied +85% from there.
   - **Mar 20 correction low** (~$60.8k): Touch of −2SD after 18% decline. Reversed sharply after FOMC.

3. **Trend-following regimes dominate when net ETF inflows > $500M/day.** In February-March, daily inflows consistently exceeded this threshold, and BTC hugged the +2SD band.

4. **Volatility regime is evolving.** The structural decline in BTC's 90-day annualized volatility (from 150%+ to <50%) suggests traditional Bollinger Band widths calibrated on earlier data may need adjustment.

5. **Halving-driven positioning** (April 20) was a latent factor throughout Q1 that may have suppressed deep selling (every dip was bought aggressively).
